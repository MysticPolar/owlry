## Summary

### Keep building when entrepreneurship shows no results yet

**What they agree on**
- Find one specific person with a real complaint and directly ask what's broken.
- Perseverance is practice against your previous work, not by counting outcomes (Seth differs).
- Name one exact change and make it this week — that's the work.

**Where they differ**
- Duckworth vs Camus on timing: write the fix before you talk to them (protect the practice) or after (serve the complaint first). Both get written; the order decides whether discipline or listening leads.

**What fits your situation**
Seth identifies the first move: find one person, ask what's broken, fix it this week. Camus prevents despair by treating that complaint as a patient, not proof. Duckworth closes the loop: write what you changed, so deliberate practice stays visible.

**One next step**
Find one real person this week. Ask what's broken in your work. Write the one change. Make it.

**The books behind the conversation**
- *This Is Marketing* — Seth Godin · Names the smallest viable market: one real person, one real promise kept.
- *Grit: The Power of Passion and Perseverance* — Angela Duckworth · Shows how deliberate practice against weakness, not just effort, builds real perseverance.
- *The Myth of Sisyphus* — Albert Camus · Argues the work itself contains the meaning; victory is not required for perseverance.
