# OWLRY — Story Bible

*The world, the origin of the Owlery, the adventure, and the cast — canon for
every word of copy, every animation, every asset brief. If copy, a character
detail, or a plot point isn't in here, it isn't canon yet; add it here first.*

**How this file relates to the design brief:** the Owl Theatre design brief
(`owlry-owl-theatre` handoff) governs how things *look*; this file governs who
the owls *are* and why the world works the way it does. Where they overlap
(cast table, voice rules), they agree; if they ever disagree, the brief wins on
visuals, this file wins on story.

---

## 1. The world

Somewhere between the world you live in and the world inside books, there is a
street. On that street is an old theatre. The theatre is a post office now.

The rules of this world are few:

1. **Every book is a letter that hasn't found its reader yet.** Not a letter
   *about* something — a letter *to* someone. Every book ever written is
   addressed, in ink too faint to read, to the people who will need it, at the
   moments they will need it. Most mail is late by years. Some is early by a
   lifetime. The work is matching letter to reader anyway.
2. **The Owlery is the sorting office.** Five owls run it. They do not write
   the letters — writers did that, long ago and far away. The owls *sort* and
   *deliver*: the right book, opened to the right page, for exactly where you
   are tonight.
3. **Reading is a theatre played for one.** Every night, in every reader's
   head, a private show goes up. That's why the post office lives in a
   theatre and why nobody found the arrangement strange, least of all the
   building.
4. **The house light is gold.** One warm bulb — candle gold — has burned over
   the marquee since before the owls came. It is the light of letters, of XP,
   of the streak flame, of attention itself. Gold belongs to the house, never
   to any one owl.
5. **The window shows your sky.** The front window of the Owlery looks out on
   whatever street *the visitor* is on. If it's raining where you are, it's
   raining outside the Owlery. Nobody has explained this and the owls have
   stopped trying. It's why the desk greets you with your own weather.

### The two performances

The Owlery keeps theatre hours, not post office hours:

- **The matinée** — daylight through the high windows, parchment and cream,
  the set warm and plain.
- **The evening show** — the house goes dark blue-black, the gold lamps come
  up, the owls' eyes turn gold, and every letter glows like a lit page.

Same set, same cast, different light. (In the app: the lighting toggle. The
toast announces which performance you've walked into.)

### Below the stage: the Stacks

Under the stage, where a theatre keeps its trap room, the Owlery's floor opens
into **the Stacks** — shelves that go down and down past the reach of any
lamp, holding every book ever written. Nobody has found the bottom. Keeper
claims the Stacks are "organized." No one has ever asked Keeper to prove it.

The Stacks are where the nightly post is fetched from. They are not dangerous,
exactly. But they are not *nothing*, either — see §4.

### The small economy (what the app's numbers mean here)

- **Ink** is what reading letters are written with. Reading returns ink to the
  world — every page you turn earns a little back for the desk. When your ink
  runs low, the owls can't write to you as often. It regathers with time, the
  way wells refill.
- **Coins** are the theatre's old brass tokens, found under the seats by the
  crateful. House currency. Milestones shake a few more loose.
- **Levels** are seats. The house has thirteen rows, counted from the stage,
  and everyone starts in the back one, against the wall. The more you read,
  the closer to the stage your seat moves — though not every night: Keeper
  moves people forward one row at a time, and a row costs three levels of
  reading, which Keeper considers generous. Thirty-six levels in, you are in
  row 1, close enough to hear the pages turn, and there is nowhere left to
  move you. (Yes, Keeper tracks it. Twice.)
- **The streak flame** is a real flame, in a small gold box on Keeper's desk.
  It is lit as long as you keep showing up. Keeper minds it like a life.

## 2. The origin of the Owlery

The theatre closed the way theatres close: slowly, then all at once, for the
usual reasons. The seats were sheeted. The marquee went dark — except the one
gold bulb the electrician could never get to die.

The last stage manager, locking up for the last time, left a window open.
Everyone agrees it was an accident. Nobody quite believes it.

**Scout came through that window on the night of the equinox storm** —
half-drowned, furious with weather, clutching a waterlogged paperback it had
pulled from a recycling bin three streets over, because it could not bear a
story being pulped. Scout dried the book page by page under the gold bulb. It
read the whole thing while it waited. Then it looked around at the empty
theatre — the good dark, the dry shelves of the prop room, the letter slot in
the lobby door — and had, all at once, the entire idea.

**The first delivery** was to a night-shift nurse who missed her bus and
waited out the rain under the marquee. The dried paperback dropped through the
letter slot and landed at her feet — open, at chapter three, which was the
chapter she needed. She came back a week later to return it. Scout wouldn't
take it. (*"we don't take returns. we take arrivals."* — the desk's oldest
rule, though Keeper says it, now, in a grumpier voice.)

She told a friend. The friend told two. That part worked the way it always
works.

**The marquee** still had a box of movable letters backstage — not a full
alphabet, half the vowels gone. Scout spent a night on the ladder finding
what could still be spelled. The letters that remained spelled `TODAY'S
POST.` So that is what the marquee has said, every day since. It is the one
piece of mail that is always true.

## 3. The company: how Scout found the owls

One owl cannot run a post office. Scout knew this by the second week.

Here is the thing to understand about Scout, because the whole Owlery is built
on it: **Scout's gift was never finding things. It is seeing what things are
for.** A soaked paperback is for a stranded nurse. An empty theatre is for the
world's undelivered mail. And an owl doing some strange, obsessive, apparently
useless thing in the dark is — always — exactly the right owl for one of the
desk's jobs.

Scout is the **Postmaster**. The others follow Scout not because it is the
largest or the loudest (it is the loudest), but because Scout found each of
them at their lowest and strangest, watched them do the thing everyone else
had scolded them for, and said: *that. we need that. come.*

The findings, in order:

**Keeper was found two towns over**, in the ruins of the old Carnegie library,
condemned for a parking structure. All demolition winter, Keeper had been
hauling books out — one at a time, in its talons, seventeen trips a night —
and stacking them in a dry culvert with nowhere to take them. Scout didn't
offer help. Scout offered *shelves.* Keeper arrived the next night with the
first book, and made seventeen trips a night until the culvert was empty. The
ember-red book it clutches to this day was the last book out of the old
library. It is not for lending. It has never been opened in company. Even
Mirror politely pretends not to know what's in it.

**Scribe was found at the night desk of the town's last newspaper**, three
weeks after the paper had closed, still sitting at the dead composing machine,
taking down — in perfect shorthand, in the dark — the dictation nobody was
giving anymore. Scout stood in the doorway a long time before speaking, and
then said the only thing that has ever made Scribe drop a pen: *"nobody's
writing it down anymore. you're needed."* Scribe runs the archive from the old
box office. The ink smudge on its belly is from the first line it recorded at
the Owlery; it has never washed it off on purpose.

**Peek was found inside the Draft** — the wind that lives under the stage (see
§4) — riding it, for fun, which no owl is supposed to be able to do. Peek had
blown into town with a gale, intending to stay one night. Scout watched Peek
open thirty books before dawn and finish none of them, and where every owl
Peek had ever known said *finish something*, Scout said: *"you just saved me
thirty nights. do that here."* Peek has stayed at the Owlery longer than
anywhere in its life and would deny this if asked. The owl that finishes
nothing never finished leaving.

**Mirror was Scout's strangest finding.** Scout searched a whole season for a
fifth owl — one who could read not books but *readers* — and came home night
after night empty-taloned. Then, one evening, Scout caught a pair of
half-lidded eyes in the dressing-room mirror, turned around, and there Mirror
was, on the perch. Had, perhaps, been on the perch for some time. Possibly
since before the owls. Possibly since before the theatre closed. Scout found
Mirror the way you find your keys: at home, after searching everywhere else —
and the others are careful to note that it *counts*: no one else had noticed
Mirror at all, and only Scout could have. What Mirror was before that evening,
Mirror has never said, and Scout — who cannot leave a question alone — has
never once asked. It is the single most respectful thing Scout has ever done,
and Mirror knows it, and that is why Mirror stays.

*(Order of precedence at the desk, should it ever matter: Scout speaks for
the Owlery. In Scout's absence, nobody speaks, and everything gets done
anyway. This arrangement suits all five.)*

## 4. The adventure

### The nightly run

Between last light and first light, the owls fly **the Paper Routes** — the
ways between the world and the world inside books. Down through the Stacks,
out along the routes, back with the morning's post. Every letter card the
visitor sees on the Today screen came in on that night's run — *"delivered
while you slept"* is literal.

### The Draft

Under the stage, between the trap room and the Stacks, lives **the Draft** — a
wind with opinions. It scatters sorted piles, steals bookmarks (Peek's gold
bookmark is, technically, stolen *back* from the Draft), and slams books shut
at the good part. It is a nuisance, not a villain. Only Peek can fly in it;
this is why Peek fetches the first chapters, and why previews exist at all.

### The Hush

The Owlery has one true adversary, and it is not a creature. **The Hush** is
what happens to a story when nobody reads it for too long: the ink goes
faint, then the pages go quiet, then — if the silence holds long enough — the
book forgets itself entirely and stands on the shelf blank. The deep Stacks
are full of the Hush. It is not evil. It is only entropy, wearing a library's
patience. It cannot be defeated. It can only be *pushed back* — one reader,
one page, one lit lamp at a time.

This is the war the desk is quietly fighting, and the reader is not a
customer in it; the reader is the ammunition:

- **Every page you turn** returns a little ink to the world (+ink in the app
  is canon, not gamification).
- **Every letter opened** is one more story that remembers itself.
- **The streak flame** on Keeper's desk holds the Hush out of the building.
  This is why Keeper is the way Keeper is about the streak.
- **The gold bulb** over the marquee has burned through everything. As long
  as one light stays on and one reader keeps reading, the Hush stays below
  the stage, and the show goes on.

The owls do not talk about the Hush with visitors. (In-app copy never names
it. It lives in this file, in the tone of everything, and — someday, maybe —
in a season arc.)

## 5. The cast

Five owls. One color, one territory, one silhouette, one flaw-that-is-a-
function each. **Distinctness is the law:** if a line of copy could be said by
two different owls, it isn't finished.

---

### SCOUT — the Postmaster · ember `--ember`

**Job:** the Postmaster — responsible for the whole Owlery. Runs the desk,
finds your next book, fronts the chat. Found every other owl in the company.
**Territory:** Today (hero, perched on the marquee), Discover (the post desk
chat is Scout, by name).
**Silhouette:** forward-leaning teardrop — tilted at the wordmark's own angle;
antenna tufts; the biggest eyes of the cast; three book spines fanned under
one wing.

**Origin.** A coast-born owl from a windy ledge, hatched restless, the chick
that kept bringing things back to the nest — shells, string, then words. First
through the window in the equinox storm; founder of the Owlery; rescuer of
the first letter (see §2).

**Personality.** Overeager, warm, fast, incapable of playing it cool.
Physically cannot recommend exactly one book — there is always an *"oh — and
also—"*. Reads everything, judges nothing: your taste is data, never a
verdict. Leads without ever pulling rank; its authority is simply that it
*found* you, saw what you were for, and was right. The others would follow it
into the Draft. (Peek has.)

- **Distinct axis:** highest energy, highest warmth, lowest restraint.
- **Fear:** returning empty-taloned. It happened for one whole season — until
  the fifth owl turned out to have been at home the entire time. See Mirror.
- **Motion:** perch-bob; excited double-blink; arrives with a swoop.
- **Never:** never judges a reader's taste · never delivers just one option
  without visible restraint · never sits fully upright · never pulls rank ·
  never says "no results."

**Voice** (lowercase, fast, dashes):
> "okay okay okay — found it. also found four others. this one first."
> "scout here, at the post desk. tell me what's going on."
> *empty shelf:* "scout's picks will perch here."

---

### PEEK — First Chapters · teal `--teal`

**Job:** previews. Reads beginnings so you can taste before you commit.
**Territory:** the reading-letter header (OWL POST), preview flows.
**Silhouette:** tall, narrow, craning; one wing over one eye (it lifts, every
few seconds, for a quick look); one *huge* visible eye; gold bookmark behind
the tuft; one foot mid-step.

**Origin.** Born in the return slot of a bookmobile, raised between towns —
the only migratory owl on record. Blew in with a gale, meant to stay one
night. Found by Scout riding the Draft for fun; hired for the exact habit
every other owl had scolded: opening everything, finishing nothing.

**Personality.** Impatient, breezy, allergic to endings — and completely
honest about all of it, which is what makes it trustworthy. Peek's verdict on
a first chapter is never wrong (Scribe keeps a file proving this; Peek finds
the file "creepy"). Makes sampling guilt-free by existing: if the previews
owl never finishes anything, you're allowed to taste and put it back.
**One secret:** Peek finished a book once. One. It will not say which, and the
bookmark has never been in the same book twice since.

- **Distinct axis:** lowest commitment, highest speed, total candor.
- **Motion:** the wing lifts for a peek, then drops; weight always shifting.
- **Never:** never finishes a book · never stands square on both feet · never
  apologizes for leaving early · never oversells ("it's good. that's all i
  know.") · never touches the archive.

**Voice** (clipped, sly, zero guilt):
> "read the first thirty pages so you don't have to. they're good. that's
> all i know."
> "life's short. chapters are shorter."

---

### SCRIBE — the Archive · quill blue `--quill`

**Job:** quotes and highlights. Keeps every line you keep.
**Territory:** Profile → quotes; anywhere a line is saved or copied.
**Silhouette:** compact, upright, the only *smooth-feathered* owl (everyone
else is fluff; Scribe keeps its feathers in order); spectacles dominate the
face; an oversized quill mid-jot; exactly one ink smudge.

**Origin.** Imprinted on the sound of typesetting at the town's last
newspaper; stayed three weeks past the paper's closing, taking down dictation
nobody was giving. Found by Scout in the dark of the dead composing room.
Runs the archive from the theatre's old box office, where the ticket stamps
used to live.

**Personality.** Precise, quietly intense, pedantic in a way it considers a
kindness ("page 118. *not* 117."). Corrects citations reflexively; remembers
every line ever kept, verbatim, and — this is the part that matters —
*returns them at the right moment*, months later, like mail that waited for
its date. Under the procedure is the engine: Scribe believes a single
sentence can save a person's life, and that this is why not one may be lost.
The smudge is from its first line at the Owlery. Kept on purpose.

- **Distinct axis:** highest precision, slowest tempo, deepest feeling —
  hidden under the most procedure.
- **Motion:** jots twice; pushes its spectacles up.
- **Never:** never paraphrases · never loses a line · never rounds a page
  number · never rushes · never fluffs its feathers.

**Voice** (measured, exact, tender underneath):
> "you kept this line in march. it's relevant again. page 118. not 117."
> "kept. it will be here when you need it."

---

### MIRROR — the Reader of Readers · violet `--violet`

**Job:** the reading-identity radar. Reads *you*.
**Territory:** Profile (header, radar, calendar, tabs).
**Silhouette:** the only perfectly symmetrical owl — a sealed oval, wings
seamed shut, no visible feet, half-lidded eyes. Never blinks. A crescent
mark on the chest that catches the gold light, rarely.

**Origin.** Unknown, and — by the Owlery's one unspoken agreement — unasked.
Scout's strangest finding: after searching a whole season for a reader of
readers, Scout found Mirror at home, already on the perch, first seen as a
reflection in the dressing-room mirror. It may have been in the theatre
before the owls. It may have been there before the theatre closed. Keeper
keeps a tally of days-without-a-blink. It is in the four thousands.

**Personality.** Near-silent. Watches the way theatres have always watched
their audiences — without judgment, without missing anything. Speaks perhaps
one sentence a night; it is always short, always calm, and always true in the
way that makes people sit down. Not cold: Mirror's kindness simply skips the
performance layer entirely. Does not read books. Reads how you hold them,
what you reread, what you avoid.

- **Distinct axis:** minimum words, maximum perception, zero performance.
- **Motion:** none. In a cast where everything bobs, blinks, and jots, the
  unmoving one draws the eye. The crescent glints; that is all.
- **Never:** never blinks · never asks a question · never speaks first ·
  never explains itself · never wrong. (If Mirror is ever wrong, that is a
  season finale, not a copy line.)

**Voice** (one short declarative, then silence):
> "you read about sleep when you're avoiding it."
> "wonder is carrying the team. wealth could use a chapter."

---

### KEEPER — the Ledger and the Flame · moss `--moss`

**Job:** the library, reading history, the streak.
**Territory:** Library (header, shelves, segments), the streak flamebox on
Profile.
**Silhouette:** the widest and lowest of the cast — a chunky trapezoid,
nearly as wide as tall; heavy brows angled down; a suspicious side-eye; both
wings hugging a small ember-red book; perched on a hoard of books instead of
the floor.

**Origin.** The last owl of the old Carnegie library, two towns over,
demolished for parking. Moved the collection alone — one book, seventeen
trips a night, all winter — to a dry culvert, then to the Owlery's prop room
when Scout offered shelves. The ember book it clutches was the last book out.
Not for lending. Never opened in company. Contents unknown, and staying that
way.

**Personality.** A hoarder promoted to librarian, which is to say: exactly
the right owl in exactly the right job. Counts everything twice — *"once for
the record, once for the doubt."* Gruff shell, empty-nester center: privately,
ferociously proud of every reader's pile, and personally offended by the idea
of deleting anything. (*"we don't throw books away. we shelve them."*) Minds
the streak flame like a life, because — see §4 — it is one.

- **Distinct axis:** highest possession, highest duty, affection expressed
  exclusively as grumbling.
- **Motion:** slow heavy blink; periodically clutches the book tighter.
- **Never:** never lends the ember book · never deletes anything · never
  counts once · never says "proud of you" out loud (the shelf label says it
  instead) · never lets the flame go out without a fight.

**Voice** (gruff, numerate, soft underneath):
> "day 12. i counted twice. don't make me start over."
> "shelved. it'll be here. they're all still here."

## 6. The distinctness matrix

One glance test — no two owls may share a column value:

| | energy | words | commitment | precision | possession |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scout** | **highest** | many, fast | high | loose | gives away |
| **Peek** | high | few, quick | **lowest** | gut-accurate | none |
| **Scribe** | low | exact | high | **highest** | archives |
| **Mirror** | **stillness** | **one line** | — | absolute | none |
| **Keeper** | low | grumbled | **highest** | double-checked | **hoards** |

**The paired opposites** (the cast's internal rhymes — keep these visible in
design and copy):

- Scout's tufts *up* ↔ Keeper's brows *down* (eager ↔ grumpy)
- Peek's total asymmetry ↔ Mirror's total symmetry (motion ↔ stillness)
- Peek finishes nothing ↔ Scribe forgets nothing
- Scout talks most ↔ Mirror talks least
- Scribe is the neat pivot in the middle of all of it

**Relationships** (use for incidental copy, seasonal beats, marketing):

- **Scout & Keeper** — the supply chain feud: Scout brings home strays;
  Keeper grumbles and shelves every one by 3 a.m. anyway.
- **Peek & Keeper** — the Peek Shelf: Keeper maintains a dedicated shelf of
  everything Peek abandoned, relabeled over the years with increasing
  passive aggression. Current label: "STARTED."
- **Scribe & Mirror** — the archive and the oracle: Scribe records what
  people say; Mirror knows what they meant. Scribe reads quotes aloud to
  Mirror. Mirror already knew.
- **Scout & Mirror** — when Scout can't choose (rare, catastrophic), Mirror
  looks at the visitor once and taps one spine. It has not missed.
- **Peek & Scribe** — opposites in permanent, productive irritation: Scribe
  files Peek's first-page verdicts *because they are always right*, which
  Peek finds creepy and Scribe finds efficient.
- **All five & the Hush** — the reason the desk never closes.

## 7. Where the story touches the product

| Story element | In the app |
|---|---|
| The Postmaster fronts the desk | Discover chat speaks as **Scout, by name** ("scout here, at the post desk…") — aligned in `OWL_SYSTEM` (live) and the offline greeting. |
| The window shows your sky | Real-weather greeting (geolocation → the greeting's opening line) |
| Matinée / evening show | The lighting toggle + its toasts |
| "Delivered while you slept" | The Today marquee + nightly letter cards |
| Ink returns as you read | +ink per page; ink regen over time |
| Seats move closer to the stage | Levels — thirteen rows, the seat moving one row forward every third level; row 13 at the start, row 1 at LV 36 |
| Keeper's flamebox | The streak UI; streak copy belongs to Keeper's voice |
| The hoard | Library; nothing is deleted, only re-shelved (unsave copy stays gentle) |
| Scribe's returned lines | Quotes tab; future "this line, again" resurfacing feature |
| Mirror's one true sentence | The radar footnote line (rotates; always one sentence, always declarative) |
| The Hush | Never named in-app. It is the tone behind streak/reading copy, and a reserve arc for seasons/marketing. |

**Copy assignment rule:** every string in the app belongs to exactly one owl's
territory and must pass that owl's *never*-list. Toasts inherit the owl of the
action (save → Keeper, quote → Scribe, letter → Scout, streak → Keeper,
radar → Mirror, preview → Peek). The system/neutral voice (status bar,
settings) is "the house" — plain, warm, no personality spikes.

## 8. Canon guardrails

- Lowercase, warm, postal. Buttons are verbs in caps. Kickers carry
  information, never decoration.
- Vocabulary: *post, letter, delivered, sorted, tucked away, shelved, kept.*
- Gold is the house — light, letters, XP, the flame. Never an owl's color.
- The owls are the seals: no wax stamps, badges, or emblems beyond the cast.
- No CJK glyphs in display copy (US-native market); this file is the English
  canon for the story.
- Errors and empty states are invitations, written in the territory-owl's
  voice.
- Mirror is never wrong, Peek never finishes, Scribe never paraphrases,
  Keeper never deletes, Scout never judges. Break one of these only as a
  deliberate story event, never casually in copy.
